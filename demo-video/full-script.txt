Every day, we save a little more. A quote from a browser. A link from a tab. A screenshot from a folder. A paragraph from a book. And then, mostly, we don't come back.

The problem isn't capture. It's return.

We save for a future self who never gets the file.

So we built this: a small, private place for the things you mean to keep. One inbox. Not a feed. Not a social profile. Not another place to perform.

Capture from the browser, from Obsidian, or from uTools. A selection, a whole page, a link, an image. Everything lands in the same inbox, with its source still attached. The URL, the title, the site, the moment you saved it.

When you're ready, open Triage. The assistant reads what you saved and suggests a summary, a few tags, maybe a duplicate. Apply what helps. Ignore the rest. Nothing is rewritten behind your back.

And when something no longer belongs, let it go. Delete is not a database operation. The card curls, the embers lift, and the inbox gets lighter.

Some fragments want to become something more. Turn one into a draft. The source and citation come with it. Write in Markdown. Export the file. The loop closes in something you can actually use.

Images are stored as content-addressed files. The index is SQLite. The database is one file. Media is a directory. You can copy it, inspect it, move it. The whole thing runs on one small server, behind a login. No analytics. No ads. No feed. Just your own archive, on your own terms.

Every night, a snapshot. One command pulls it home. Not a service you hope stays online. And because the backup is just a file, restoring is boring, which is exactly what you want.

A private place for what you keep. Save less. Return more.
